How it works. The simulator uses kinematic (single-scattering) diffraction theory: each layer's crystal lattice gives a hexagon of spots, and the twist rotates one hexagon against the other. The analyser finds the spots, folds their directions modulo 60°, splits them into two layers and reports the angle between them. A real-space image is first Fourier-transformed, which turns it into a computed diffraction pattern.

Limits. Spot brightness from double scattering (α) is a model setting, not physics fitted to data. Angles below about 2° are unreliable from spot directions alone. Materials whose lattice constants differ by under about 2% (MoS₂ and WS₂, graphene and hBN) cannot be told apart from spacing alone. Lattice constants are typical literature values.
